How it works

How Italian court auctions work

When a debtor cannot repay a bank or another creditor, the Court can sell their property at a public auction to recover the money. The whole process is public, regulated and supervised by a judge.

The auction, step by step

  1. 1. Valuation

    The Court appoints an expert who estimates the market value of the property in an official appraisal (perizia).

  2. 2. Publication

    A professional appointed by the Court, usually a lawyer or an accountant, publishes the sale on the official websites and on the Ministry of Justice portal.

  3. 3. Advertising period

    The auction is advertised for about 90–120 days. Offers can be up to 25% below the starting price.

  4. 4. Auction day

    The auction is fully online and lasts two days. With a single bidder, that bidder wins. With several, bids rise in steps of at least €1,000.

  5. 5. If nobody bids

    The Court sets a new auction with the minimum price cut by another 25%. This can repeat several times, which is how prices can become very low.

Example

Property valued at

€100,000

Minimum valid offer at the first auction

€75,000

Unsold? At the next auction the minimum drops again by 25%.

The deposit

To take part you pay a deposit of 10% of your offer into the Court's bank account. If you win, it is deducted from the price. If you do not win, it is refunded in full.

All payments go to the Court, never to us.

Why auctions are an opportunity for international buyers

Many Italians avoid auctions because they think the process is complicated. Italian buyers also tend to look for modern apartments in residential areas, close to family. Historic houses in old towns, country homes and mountain properties often receive no local bids, so their price keeps falling.

These are exactly the homes many foreign buyers love: character, peace and privacy, at a price that leaves room for a good renovation.
